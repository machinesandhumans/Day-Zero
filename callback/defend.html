Chapter 5 · Call back

Hang up. Call back.

The title of the video was the lesson all along. A deepfake controls the call it starts. It can't control the call you start.

The Exec · Video call04:12

Your turn. The Exec is on video. He needs a supplier's bank details changed before end of day, and he'd rather you didn't mention it to anyone. What do you do?

That's the $25 million answer

Changed bank details are one of the most common ways company money disappears. The next real invoice gets paid straight to Ryan, and nobody notices for weeks. The face was the only evidence you had, and the face is the one thing a deepfake gets right.

Good instinct. Not enough on its own.

A question saved Ferrari. But you're still on the caller's line, and a well-researched scammer may know the answer, or say "I can't talk about that now" and push harder. Use the question as a warning light, then do C anyway.

That's the move

If it's really him, you've cost him two minutes and he'll likely respect it. If it's not, the scam is over: Ryan can't answer a phone he doesn't own. Then report it, even if it turns out to be nothing.

Your toolkit

Six habits that beat a perfect fake.

01 · THE BIG ONECall back on a number you look up yourself.Use the company directory, the number saved in your phone from before, or walk over to their desk. Never the number, link or meeting invite in the message that made the request.
02Agree on a code word.For teams that move money or grant access, pick a word or question that's never written in email or chat. Do it at home too: voice-clone "family emergency" calls are common.
03Two people for money and access.Payments, bank-detail changes and password resets get a second person to approve. "Urgent" doesn't get an exception. Urgent is the reason the rule exists.
04Keep it in the normal channel.Real requests come through the usual systems. A boss on WhatsApp, a personal email or a new number is the LastPass lesson: that's enough reason to stop.
05Slow down on purpose.Pressure is the weapon. Any real leader will wait five minutes. If they won't, you've learned something.
06Report it, even after the fact.Already clicked, sent or paid? Report it right away. A wire caught in the first hours can sometimes be pulled back. A wire reported next week can't.
What to say. Copy it."Happy to help. Our policy is that I call you back on your directory number before I act on this. Talk in two minutes."

Verification isn't rude. It's the job.

No executive has ever been fired for being verified. Several have been very glad of it. If a leader gets annoyed that you followed the process, that's a conversation for them and the security team, not a reason to skip it.

Shrink your own footprint
  • Use the default voicemail greeting, not your voice.
  • Keep out-of-office replies to outsiders short: no dates, no itinerary, no backup contact.
  • Post trips after you're home. Lock personal accounts to friends.
  • On professional profiles, share your role, not your reporting chain, systems or start date.
Something feel off? Report it.

Nobody gets in trouble for a false alarm. The security team would rather check ten of them than miss the real one.
